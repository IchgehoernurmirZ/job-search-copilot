# Interview prep and debrief

## Prep for a specific interview

Gather: the tracker record (full JD, assessment, form answers), the
evidence library, the round (recruiter screen / hiring manager / panel /
case / AI video interview), interviewer names if known, and the length.

Produce one prep doc, attached to the tracker record:

1. **What they're hiring for**, ranked (reuse the tailor assessment)
2. **Likely questions**: the standard ones for the round + one per must-have
   requirement + one per gap and red flag. Put the hardest first.
3. **Answers** in the user's interview language, built from the evidence
   library only, using the structure below
4. **The gap answers**: honest, short, with what they'd do in the first
   weeks to close it. For a tool they haven't used hands-on: say they've
   studied it, then show they understand how it works; never claim usage.
5. **Logistics answers, said in one go**: salary expectation (anchor from
   `profile.md`, the posted band and the gap between them), start date,
   work authorization, location / hybrid. Leaving these vague reads as
   hesitation.
6. **Three questions to ask them**, aimed at the red flags
7. A 60-second "tell me about yourself": who they are in one line, the two
   most relevant experiences with one number each, one line on why this
   company

### Answer structure

**Default to a short version and stop.** About 20 seconds: the conclusion
plus one concrete detail, ending with a hook like "I can walk through the
specifics if that's useful." Information the interviewer digs out weighs
more than information volunteered, and which story they chase tells you
what they're scoring.

Give the full version straight away when the question asks for it
("walk me through", "describe a time", "measurable impact") or on
logistics questions.

**Full version (2 to 3 minutes):** conclusion → approach → actions with at
least two real obstacles in them → what went wrong / what they'd do
differently now → takeaway linked back to this role.

A good failure: their own judgment was wrong, they changed it, and the
change produced a result or a lasting rule. Not a failure: a difficult
client, a cut budget, a teammate who didn't deliver. If the library has no
real failure for a story, ask the user; never invent one.

**Style rules:**
- First sentence is the answer. No "it depends", no "great question".
- Don't announce structure ("Two things...") and don't use slogan lines.
  It should sound like someone thinking, not a speech.
- Every story names the actual thing: the product, the tool, what the email
  said, the number.
- Repeat the noun from the question at least once (asked about conversion
  rate → say "conversion rate").
- When unsure, give a confidence level instead of "or": "Option two, about
  seventy percent sure."
- "This was my first time" is fine if followed by "but the way I approached
  it was...".

Save reusable answers to a general question bank (no company specifics):
a Notion page if that is their home base, otherwise a `## Question bank`
section at the end of `job-search-workspace.md`. Keep company-specific
prep on that application's record.

## Debrief after the interview

If there's a recording, transcript or meeting notes (e.g. a meeting-notes
tool or connector), read those instead of asking the user to recount it.
Otherwise ask for a quick dump: questions asked, where they got stuck,
anything the interviewer said about next steps.

Write a debrief on the tracker record:

- **Questions asked** vs. what the prep predicted (hit rate)
- **What went well**, specifically
- **Where they got stuck**, and why: missing material, no structure,
  language, nerves, didn't know the domain term. For each, the fix and a
  better version of the answer.
- **Phrasing upgrades**: "you said X → could say Y", split into
  role-specific (stays on the record) and general (goes into the question
  bank)
- **Signals** about the role, team or process learned in the interview
- **Next step** and date; update the tracker status

Keep it kind and concrete. One or two changes they'll actually make beat
ten they won't.
